The template. Not for its results, but for its shape.

**Why it matters structurally.** HiKE demonstrates that a code-switching *evaluation
framework* — a hierarchy of switch granularity plus an explicit loanword policy — is
enough of a contribution for Findings of EACL. That answers a question I had been circling:
whether "we built a better metric" is a paper or a section. It is a paper, if the metric
reorders systems and the human validation is real.

**The discipline I am stealing.** The loanword labels. In Hinglish the loanword problem is
worse than in Korean-English: *policy*, *premium*, *claim* and *nominee* are the working
vocabulary of the domain and are spoken as Hindi words by Hindi speakers. Whether they
count as a code-switch changes the metric, so the policy has to be declared, not assumed.

**The gap it leaves me.** Korean-English, clean recordings, not telephony, not entity-focused.
Replicating the structure for Hinglish over 8 kHz telephony with BFSI entity weighting is a
real paper and this one shows how to shape it.
